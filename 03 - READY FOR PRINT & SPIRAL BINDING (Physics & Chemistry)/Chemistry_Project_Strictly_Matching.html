AIM & OBJECTIVE

AIM OF THE EXPERIMENT

To study and compare the foaming capacity of five different commercial bathing soaps and to investigate the effect of water hardness on their lather formation.

SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES

  • To prepare identical 0.5% aqueous solutions of five popular commercial soaps: Dove, Lux, Santoor, Dettol, and Lifebuoy.
  • To measure and compare the initial foam height produced by each soap solution upon standard agitation in distilled water.
  • To record the time taken for the generated foam to completely subside and disappear.
  • To evaluate the adverse effect of calcium and magnesium salts (hard water) on the foaming capacity of each soap sample.
  • To correlate the observed foaming performance with the chemical composition and Total Fatty Matter (TFM) of each brand.

CHEMISTRY OF SOAPS

Soaps are water-soluble cleansing agents used by human civilization for thousands of years. Chemically, soaps are sodium or potassium salts of higher fatty acids containing 12 to 18 carbon atoms, such as sodium stearate (C₁₇H₃₅COONa), sodium palmitate (C₁₅H₃₁COONa), and sodium oleate (C₁₇H₃₃COONa).

SAPONIFICATION REACTION

Soaps are prepared by the alkaline hydrolysis of natural vegetable oils or animal fats (esters of glycerol known as triglycerides) with aqueous sodium hydroxide (NaOH) or potassium hydroxide (KOH):

Triglyceride (Fat/Oil) + 3 NaOH → Glycerol + 3 RCOONa (Soap)

After hydrolysis, common salt (NaCl) is added to precipitate soap from the solution. This process is called salting out of soap. Glycerol remains in the aqueous layer and is recovered as a valuable byproduct.

7

SURFACE CHEMISTRY

SURFACE TENSION & FOAM FORMATION

Pure water has high surface tension (~72 dynes/cm), making it difficult for water alone to wet oily surfaces or produce lather. Soap molecules act as surfactants (surface active agents) that migrate to the air-water interface and significantly lower the surface tension of water.

When the soap solution is agitated vigorously, air is entrapped in the liquid. Due to reduced surface tension, thin liquid films (lamellae) are formed, creating a colloidal dispersion of gas in liquid called foam or lather.

TOTAL FATTY MATTER (TFM)

According to the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), toilet soaps are graded based on their Total Fatty Matter (TFM) percentage:

  • Grade 1 (TFM ≥ 76%): Premium bathing soaps offering rich, dense, and long-lasting lather with excellent skin feel.
  • Grade 2 (TFM ≥ 70%): Medium-grade commercial toilet soaps.
  • Grade 3 (TFM ≥ 60%): Utility and antiseptic soaps with higher filler content.

CLEANSING MECHANISM

A soap molecule possesses two distinct chemical ends: a non-polar hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail and a polar hydrophilic carboxylate head (-COO⁻Na⁺):

Figure 1: Soap molecular structure, micelle formation, and scum reaction in hard water.

In water, soap forms spherical clusters called micelles. The non-polar tails point inwards, trapping greasy dirt particles, while polar heads face outward into water. Agitation lifts dirt off surfaces into suspension.

PROCEDURE

The practical investigation was carried out step-by-step according to the following procedure:

  • Step 1: Weigh accurately 0.50 g of each soap sample (Dove, Lux, Santoor, Dettol, Lifebuoy) using a sensitive chemical balance.
  • Step 2: Transfer each 0.50 g sample into a separate labeled 250 mL beaker. Add 100 mL of warm distilled water to each beaker.
  • Step 3: Stir thoroughly with clean glass rods and gently warm the solutions to ensure complete dissolution, forming 0.5% (w/v) soap stock solutions.
  • Step 4: Take 5 identical graduated test tubes labeled 1 to 5. Measure and transfer exactly 10 mL of each soap solution into its corresponding test tube.
  • Step 5: Stopper Test Tube 1 firmly. Shake it vigorously in a vertical motion for exactly 15 seconds (20 uniform strokes).
  • Step 6: Place the test tube in the stand immediately. Start the stopwatch and record the initial foam height in centimeters using a ruler.
  • Step 7: Stop the timer when the foam layer completely subsides and only liquid remains. Record the time in seconds.
  • Step 8: Repeat steps 5 to 7 for all 5 soaps in distilled water, and then repeat with 5 mL of hard water (CaCl₂) added.

DATA ANALYSIS

From the experimental observation tables, several important chemical insights emerge:

  • Comparative Foaming Order: In distilled water, the foaming capacity follows the sequence: Dove > Lux > Santoor > Dettol > Lifebuoy. Dove produces the highest initial foam height (8.5 cm) and the longest persistence (185 s).
  • Role of Fatty Acid Composition & TFM: Premium toilet soaps like Dove and Lux contain higher Total Fatty Matter (TFM > 75%) and potassium/sodium salts of saturated fatty acids with moisturizing agents. These stabilize the thin liquid films (lamellae) of bubbles, prolonging foam life.
  • Antiseptic Soaps Performance: Soaps like Dettol and Lifebuoy contain antiseptic additives (chloroxylenol, terpineol) and lower TFM (~60%). These additives disrupt foam stability, leading to quicker foam collapse.
  • Effect of Hard Water: The addition of calcium chloride caused an immediate 55–65% reduction in foam height across all brands, along with insoluble curdy precipitate (scum). The Ca²⁺ ions react with stearate ions, removing active soap from solution.
12

RESULTS & CONCLUSIONS

EXPERIMENTAL INFERENCES

  • The foaming capacity of the five commercial soap samples tested in distilled water ranks in the following decreasing order:
    Dove > Lux > Santoor > Dettol > Lifebuoy
  • Dove exhibited the highest foaming capacity, producing 8.5 cm of foam persisting for 185 seconds.
  • Lifebuoy exhibited the lowest foaming capacity among tested brands, producing 5.4 cm of foam subsiding within 102 seconds.
  • Hard water containing dissolved calcium ions severely hampers lather formation by precipitating soap as insoluble calcium stearate:
    2 C₁₇H₃₅COONa + Ca²⁺ → (C₁₇H₃₅COO)₂Ca ↓ + 2 Na⁺
  • Synthetic detergents or water-softening agents (such as Na₂CO₃) must be used with hard water to achieve efficient cleansing.

PRECAUTIONS & ERRORS

PRECAUTIONS TAKEN

  • Use identical graduated test tubes with the same internal bore and length to ensure equal surface area.
  • Weigh the soap samples accurately on an analytical balance to maintain exact 0.5% concentration.
  • Ensure complete dissolution of the soap in warm distilled water before transferring to test tubes.
  • Agitate each test tube with the same number of vigorous strokes (20 shakes in 15 seconds) with uniform amplitude.
  • Measure foam height immediately after shaking to prevent pre-measurement subsidence error.

SOURCES OF ERROR

  • Slight variation in manual shaking force between successive test tubes.
  • Inaccuracy in reading the meniscus boundary between liquid and bubble froth.
  • Small temperature variations in soap solutions during the experiment.
