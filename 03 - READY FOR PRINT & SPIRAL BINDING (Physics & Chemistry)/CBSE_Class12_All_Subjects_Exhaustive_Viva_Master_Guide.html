⚡ SECTION 1: PHYSICS — ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION (EMI & LENZ'S LAW) 25 Core Questions
📌 HIGH-YIELD FORMULAS & UNITS AT A GLANCE: NCERT Chapter 6
• Magnetic Flux: Φ = B · A · cos(θ) | Unit: Weber (Wb) = T·m² | Scalar quantity
• Faraday's Law: e = - N · (dΦ / dt) | Induced Current: I = e / R = - (N / R) · (dΦ / dt)
• Induced Charge: q = ΔΦ / R (Independent of time / speed!)
• Self-Inductance: L = μ₀ μr (N² A / l) | Unit: Henry (H) = V·s / A
• Motional EMF: e = B · v · l · sin(θ)
Q1. What is electromagnetic induction (EMI)? Who discovered it?
Electromagnetic induction is the phenomenon of producing an electromotive force (EMF) and electric current in a closed circuit whenever the magnetic flux linked with the circuit changes over time. Discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831.
Q2. State Faraday's First and Second Laws of EMI.
First Law: Whenever magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes, an EMF is induced in it, lasting only as long as the change continues.
Second Law: The magnitude of induced EMF is directly proportional to the time rate of change of magnetic flux: e = - N (dΦ/dt).
Q3. State Lenz's Law and explain its relation to Conservation of Energy.
Statement: The polarity of induced EMF is always such that it produces a current which opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it.
Conservation of Energy: When an incoming North pole approaches a coil, an opposing North pole is induced, repelling it. Mechanical work done against this repulsion converts directly into electrical energy. If it attracted instead, energy would be created from nothing, violating physics laws.
Q4. Does induced charge depend on the speed of the magnet? (EXAMINER TRAP)
NO! Induced EMF and induced current depend on speed (rate dt), but total induced charge does NOT: q = ΔΦ / R. It depends strictly on total flux change and circuit resistance.
Q5. What happens when a magnet falls through a vertical copper pipe?
Changing flux generates circulating eddy currents in the copper pipe walls. By Lenz's law, these exert an upward opposing magnetic force on the magnet. The magnet accelerates at a < g, quickly reaches a constant terminal velocity, and falls much slower than free fall.
Q6. What are Eddy currents? Mention one useful and one harmful effect.
Circulating loops of current induced within bulk conductors exposed to changing magnetic fields.
• Useful: Electromagnetic damping in galvanometers, magnetic brakes in high-speed trains, induction cooktops.
• Harmful: Heat dissipation (I²R loss) in transformer iron cores; minimized by using laminated thin sheets insulated by varnish.
Q7. Why is soft iron chosen as the core material rather than steel?
Soft iron has very high magnetic permeability (μr > 1000) that concentrates magnetic lines of force. It also has low retentivity and low coercivity, resulting in a narrow hysteresis loop with negligible heating loss.
Q8. What is a center-zero galvanometer? Why use it here?
It has its zero marking at the center of the scale with tick marks on both sides (-30 to +30). It detects micro-level currents and shows current direction based on whether the needle deflects left or right.
🧪 SECTION 2: CHEMISTRY — FOAMING CAPACITY OF COMMERCIAL SOAPS 25 Core Questions
📌 HIGH-YIELD REACTIONS & STANDARDS AT A GLANCE: Surface Chemistry & Soaps
• Saponification: Triglyceride + 3 NaOH → Glycerol (Propane-1,2,3-triol) + 3 RCOONa (Soap)
• Hard Water Scum: 2 C₁₇H₃₅COONa + Ca²⁺ → (C₁₇H₃₅COO)₂Ca ↓ (White Curd) + 2 Na⁺
• TFM Grades (BIS): Grade 1 (≥ 76% TFM, best lather), Grade 2 (≥ 70%), Grade 3 (≥ 60%)
• Ranking in Distilled Water: Dove > Lux > Santoor > Dettol > Lifebuoy
Q9. What is a soap chemically? Give two examples.
Soaps are water-soluble sodium or potassium salts of higher fatty acids (C12 to C18). Examples: Sodium Stearate (C₁₇H₃₅COONa) and Sodium Palmitate (C₁₅H₃₁COONa).
Q10. Why is NaCl added after saponification? What is this called?
Called Salting Out of Soap. Adding NaCl increases Na⁺ ion concentration. By the Common Ion Effect, the ionic product exceeds the solubility product (Ksp), precipitating soap out of solution.
Q11. What is the byproduct of soap manufacture and what is its IUPAC name?
Glycerol (IUPAC: Propane-1,2,3-triol). Used in cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, moisturizers, and for making nitroglycerine explosives.
Q12. Explain the dual hydrophobic/hydrophilic structure of soap.
• Hydrophobic Tail: Long non-polar hydrocarbon chain (C₁₇H₃₅-) that dissolves in oil, grease, and dirt.
• Hydrophilic Head: Polar carboxylate group (-COO⁻Na⁺) that interacts with polar water molecules.
Q13. What is a micelle? Define Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC).
A micelle is a spherical colloidal cluster where hydrophobic tails face inward trapping grease and hydrophilic heads face outward towards water. CMC is the minimum surfactant concentration above which micelles spontaneously form (~10⁻³ M for soap).
Q14. Why does soap fail in hard water?
Hard water contains Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions which react with soap to form insoluble sticky scum: 2 C₁₇H₃₅COONa + Ca²⁺ → (C₁₇H₃₅COO)₂Ca ↓ + 2 Na⁺. Soap is consumed as precipitate before lather can form.
Q15. Why did Dove show the highest foaming capacity?
Dove contains high Total Fatty Matter (>75%), incorporates potassium/sodium cocoyl isethionate, and includes 1/4th moisturizing cream, creating elastic bubble films (lamellae) that resist drainage.

Q17. (English) How does blue light disrupt adolescent sleep?
Blue light (450–480 nm) stimulates melanopsin retinal cells, sending signals to the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) that suppress the pineal gland's secretion of melatonin (the sleep hormone), delaying sleep onset.
Q18. (IP) Difference between Pandas Series and DataFrame?
A Series is 1-dimensional with a single data column and index. A DataFrame is a 2-dimensional size-mutable tabular structure with labeled rows and columns.
Q19. (IP) Difference between WHERE and HAVING clause in SQL?
WHERE filters individual records before any grouping occurs and cannot contain aggregate functions. HAVING filters aggregated groups after the GROUP BY clause and operates on aggregates (e.g. HAVING AVG(marks) > 75).
Q20. (IP) Difference between DROP, DELETE, and TRUNCATE?
DELETE (DML) removes specific rows and can be rolled back. TRUNCATE (DDL) rapidly deallocates all table rows but keeps structure. DROP (DDL) completely deletes table structure, indexes, and data permanently.
