SECTION 1: PHYSICS — ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION (EMI & LENZ'S LAW) 25 Core Questions
Q1. What is electromagnetic induction (EMI)? Who discovered it?
Electromagnetic induction is the phenomenon of generating an electromotive force (EMF) and electric current in a closed circuit whenever the magnetic flux linked with that circuit changes over time. It was discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831 (and independently by Joseph Henry).
Q2. What is magnetic flux? What is its formula and SI unit? Is it scalar or vector?
Magnetic flux (Φ) represents the total number of magnetic field lines passing perpendicularly through a given surface area.
Φ = B · A = B · A · cos(θ)
SI unit: Weber (Wb) or Tesla-meter² (T·m²). CGS unit: Maxwell (1 Wb = 10⁸ Maxwell). Magnetic flux is a scalar quantity (dot product of two vectors).
Q3. State Faraday's First and Second Laws of Electromagnetic Induction.
First Law: Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a closed conducting circuit changes, an induced EMF is produced in the circuit, which lasts only as long as the flux continues to change.
Second Law: The magnitude of the induced EMF is directly proportional to the time rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the circuit:
e = - N · (dΦ / dt)
Q4. What does the negative sign signify in Faraday's equation e = -N(dΦ/dt)?
The negative sign represents Lenz's Law. It indicates that the polarity of the induced EMF is always such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that causes it.
Q5. State Lenz's Law and explain its relationship with the Law of Conservation of Energy.
Statement: The polarity of an induced EMF always produces an electric current whose magnetic field opposes the change in magnetic flux producing it.
Conservation of Energy: When a magnet's North pole approaches a coil, an induced North pole appears on the coil face to repel it. The mechanical work done by the experimenter against this repulsive force is converted into electrical energy in the coil. If the induced pole attracted instead, the magnet would accelerate without any external work, creating energy from nothing, which violates the Law of Conservation of Energy.
Q6. Does induced charge depend on the speed at which the magnet is moved? (EXAMINER TRAP)
NO! This is a classic board trap. Induced current (i = e/R) and induced EMF depend directly on speed (time rate dt), but total induced charge does NOT:
q = ∫ i dt = (1/R) ∫ (dΦ/dt) dt = ΔΦ / R
Induced charge depends only on total flux change ΔΦ and circuit resistance R, completely independent of how fast or slow the magnet moves!
Q7. What happens if the coil circuit is open? Is EMF induced? Is current induced?
Induced EMF is still produced across the open terminals because flux change continues. However, induced current is zero because there is no closed conductive path for charges to circulate.
Q8. What happens when a magnet is dropped through a long vertical copper tube?
As the magnet falls, changing magnetic flux induces circulating eddy currents in the copper wall. By Lenz's law, these eddy currents exert an upward opposing magnetic force on the falling magnet. Consequently, the magnet initially accelerates at a < g, and quickly reaches a constant terminal velocity, falling slower than in free fall.
Q9. What are Eddy Currents (Foucault Currents)? Mention two useful and two harmful applications.
Eddy currents are circulating loops of electrical current induced within bulk metallic conductors when exposed to changing magnetic fields.
Useful: (1) Electromagnetic damping in dead-beat galvanometers; (2) Induction furnaces for melting metals; (3) Magnetic brakes in electric trains.
Harmful: Unwanted heat dissipation (Joule heating loss I²Rt) in transformer iron cores and motor armatures. Reduced by using laminated thin sheets insulated by varnish.
Q10. Define Self-Inductance (L) and Mutual Inductance (M). What is their SI unit?
Self-Inductance (L): The property of a coil by which it opposes any change in current flowing through itself by inducing a back EMF: e = -L (di/dt).
Mutual Inductance (M): The property of a pair of coils by which a change of current in the primary coil induces an EMF in the secondary coil: e₂ = -M (di₁/dt).
SI unit: Henry (H). 1 Henry = 1 Volt·second / Ampere (V·s/A).
Q11. Why is soft iron used as the core in coils and transformers instead of steel?
Soft iron has very high magnetic permeability (μr > 1000), which strongly concentrates magnetic flux. It also possesses low retentivity and very low coercivity, resulting in a narrow hysteresis loop with minimal energy loss per cycle of magnetization. Steel has high coercivity and causes severe hysteresis heating loss.
Q12. What is the difference between Fleming's Left-Hand Rule and Right-Hand Rule?
Left-Hand Rule: Used for electric motors to determine the direction of mechanical force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field (Thumb = Force, Forefinger = Field B, Center finger = Current I).
Right-Hand Rule: Used for electric generators to determine the direction of induced current when a conductor moves in a magnetic field (Thumb = Motion v, Forefinger = Field B, Center finger = Induced Current I).
Q13. What is a center-zero galvanometer? Why did you use it instead of a regular DC ammeter?
A center-zero galvanometer has its zero point right at the center of the scale with tick marks on both left (-30) and right (+30). It detects extremely small micro-level currents (high sensitivity) and immediately reveals the direction of current flow depending on whether the needle deflects left or right. Standard DC ammeters only read unidirectional positive currents.
Q14. What is Motional EMF? Write its mathematical formula.
Motional EMF is the electromotive force induced across the ends of a conducting rod moving with velocity v through a magnetic field B:
e = B · l · v · sin(θ)
Where l is conductor length, v is velocity, and θ is the angle between velocity vector and magnetic field vector.
Q15. What is the AC frequency in Indian households and what does it mean?
In India, the standard AC supply is 50 Hz, 220 V. A frequency of 50 Hz means the alternating current completes 50 complete sinusoidal cycles per second, changing its direction of flow 100 times every second.
SECTION 2: CHEMISTRY — FOAMING CAPACITY OF COMMERCIAL SOAPS 25 Core Questions
Q16. What is a soap chemically? Give two specific chemical examples with formulas.
Soaps are water-soluble sodium or potassium salts of long-chain higher fatty acids containing 12 to 18 carbon atoms.
Examples: (1) Sodium Stearate: C₁₇H₃₅COONa; (2) Sodium Palmitate: C₁₅H₃₁COONa; (3) Sodium Oleate: C₁₇H₃₃COONa.
Q17. What is saponification? Write the complete word and chemical reaction.
Saponification is the alkaline hydrolysis of natural fats and oils (glyceryl esters of fatty acids / triglycerides) with aqueous sodium hydroxide or potassium hydroxide to yield soap and glycerol:
Triglyceride + 3 NaOH → Glycerol (Propane-1,2,3-triol) + 3 RCOONa (Soap)
(CH₂OCOR-CHOCOR-CH₂OCOR) + 3 NaOH → CH₂OH-CHOH-CH₂OH + 3 RCOONa
Q18. Why is common salt (NaCl) added after saponification? What is this process called?
Common salt is added to precipitate soap out of the aqueous solution. This process is called Salting Out of Soap. By adding NaCl, the concentration of Na⁺ ions increases sharply; due to the Common Ion Effect, the ionic product [RCOO⁻][Na⁺] exceeds the solubility product (Ksp) of soap, causing it to precipitate as a solid curd floating on top.
Q19. What is the byproduct of soap manufacture, and what is its IUPAC name and use?
The byproduct is Glycerol (or Glycerine). IUPAC name: Propane-1,2,3-triol. It is separated by fractional distillation under reduced pressure. It is used as a humectant in skin creams, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, and for making explosives (nitroglycerine).
Q20. Why are potassium soaps softer than sodium soaps? Where are they used?
Potassium (K⁺) ions are larger in ionic radius and have lower charge density than sodium (Na⁺) ions. Potassium soaps form looser crystal lattices, which makes them softer, more soluble in water, and gentler on the skin. They are used in shaving creams, toilet liquid soaps, and facial washes.
Q21. What is Total Fatty Matter (TFM)? How does it dictate soap grade and lather quality?
Total Fatty Matter (TFM) is the percentage of fatty substances that can be separated from soap by mineral acid treatment. According to Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS):
• Grade 1 (TFM ≥ 76%): Highest quality toilet soap; produces rich, dense lather, excellent moisturizing, and skin feel (e.g. Dove, Lux).
• Grade 2 (TFM ≥ 70%): Medium quality.
• Grade 3 (TFM ≥ 60%): Lower quality utility soaps with higher filler content.
Q22. Explain the dual hydrophobic and hydrophilic structure of a soap molecule.
A soap molecule (e.g., sodium stearate C₁₇H₃₅COONa) consists of two chemically distinct parts:
C₁₇H₃₅ (Hydrophobic Tail) | -COO⁻ Na⁺ (Hydrophilic Head)
• Hydrophobic (Lipophilic) Tail: Long non-polar aliphatic hydrocarbon chain that dissolves in grease, oil, and organic dirt.
• Hydrophilic (Lipophobic) Head: Polar ionic carboxylate group that forms strong dipole-ion attractions with polar water molecules.
Q23. What is a micelle? Define Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC) and Kraft Temperature.
Micelle: A spherical colloidal aggregate formed by surfactant molecules in aqueous solution where hydrophobic tails point inwards away from water, and hydrophilic heads point outwards toward water.
Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC): The minimum concentration of surfactant above which micelle formation begins (typically ~10⁻³ mol/L for soaps). Below CMC, soap acts as normal electrolyte.
Kraft Temperature (Tk): The minimum temperature above which micelle formation becomes thermodynamically spontaneous.
Q24. Why does soap fail to lather in hard water? Give the exact chemical equation.
Hard water contains dissolved calcium (Ca²⁺) and magnesium (Mg²⁺) ions. When soap is added, it reacts with these divalent ions to form insoluble, sticky precipitates known as scum or curd:
2 C₁₇H₃₅COO⁻Na⁺ + Ca²⁺ → (C₁₇H₃₅COO)₂Ca ↓ (Insoluble Scum) + 2 Na⁺
Soap is consumed in precipitating Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ ions rather than forming lather, drastically reducing foaming capacity until all hardness ions are precipitated.
Q25. How are synthetic detergents superior to soaps? What is their chemical structure?
Synthetic detergents are sodium salts of long-chain alkyl hydrogen sulfates (e.g., sodium lauryl sulfate C₁₂H₂₅OSO₃Na) or alkyl benzene sulfonates (e.g., sodium dodecylbenzene sulfonate). Unlike soaps, their calcium and magnesium salts are completely soluble in water, so they lather freely even in hard water and acidic solutions.
Q26. Why are modern synthetic detergents designed with straight unbranched hydrocarbon chains?
Early synthetic detergents had highly branched hydrocarbon chains that bacteria in sewage treatment plants could not metabolize, resulting in severe water pollution and foaming in rivers. Modern detergents feature straight (linear) alkyl chains, which microorganisms easily degrade, making them biodegradable.
Q27. Why did Dove show the highest foaming capacity in your experimental trial?
Dove contains high Total Fatty Matter (>75%), incorporates potassium/sodium cocoyl isethionate and sodium stearate blends, and includes 1/4th moisturizing cream. These fatty acid derivatives drastically reduce the surface tension of water and create highly stable, resilient liquid bubble walls (lamellae) that resist drainage and evaporation.
Q28. Why is the aqueous solution of soap slightly basic (alkaline)?
Soap is a salt formed by a weak acid (fatty acid RCOOH) and a strong base (NaOH). When dissolved in water, the carboxylate anion undergoes anionic hydrolysis:
RCOO⁻ + H₂O ⇔ RCOOH + OH⁻
The release of free hydroxyl ions (OH⁻) makes the solution alkaline (pH ~8 to 9.5), which turns red litmus blue.

Q32. What is 'Phantom Vibration Syndrome'?
Phantom Vibration Syndrome is a sensory hallucination where an individual mistakenly perceives that their smartphone is vibrating or ringing in their pocket or nearby when no notification has actually occurred. It stems from heightened neurological alertness and conditioned psychological anticipation.
Q33. How does smartphone blue light physically interfere with adolescent sleep cycles?
Smartphones emit short-wavelength blue light (450–480 nm) that activates melanopsin-containing retinal ganglion cells in the eyes. This sends signals to the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the brain, suppressing the pineal gland's secretion of melatonin, the sleep hormone. This delays sleep onset and degrades REM sleep architecture.
Q34. What psychological mechanism drives endless scrolling on social media platforms?
Social media apps exploit intermittent variable reward schedules (the same psychological reinforcement mechanism used in slot machines). Because users do not know whether the next swipe will deliver a stimulating meme, like, or video, dopamine is released in anticipation, creating compulsive behavioral loops.
Q35. What concrete solutions did your project recommend for students preparing for board exams?
1. Setting strict daily app limits (under 60 min for entertainment).
2. Implementing a 45-minute screen-free buffer before sleeping.
3. Switching phone screens to grayscale mode to eliminate hyper-stimulating visual colors.
4. Keeping smartphones in another room during focused study sessions.
SECTION 4: INFORMATICS PRACTICES (IP - 065) 20 Core Questions
Q36. What is the fundamental difference between a Pandas Series and a DataFrame?
• Pandas Series: A one-dimensional labeled array capable of holding any data type. It possesses a single data column with an explicit index.
• Pandas DataFrame: A two-dimensional size-mutable tabular data structure with labeled axes (rows and columns), resembling a database table or spreadsheet.
Q37. Differentiate between loc[] and iloc[] indexing in Pandas.
• loc[]: Label-based indexing; accesses rows and columns by explicit label names (e.g., df.loc['row1', 'Marks']). The endpoint is inclusive.
• iloc[]: Integer position-based indexing; accesses rows and columns by 0-based integer coordinates (e.g., df.iloc[0, 2]). The endpoint is exclusive.
Q38. What is the difference between SQL WHERE clause and HAVING clause?
• WHERE: Filters individual rows before any grouping or aggregation takes place. Cannot contain aggregate functions (e.g. WHERE marks > 80).
• HAVING: Filters aggregated groups after the GROUP BY operation has been computed. Can and usually does contain aggregate functions (e.g. HAVING AVG(marks) > 75).
Q39. Differentiate between DROP, DELETE, and TRUNCATE in SQL.
• DELETE (DML): Deletes specific rows satisfying a condition; can be rolled back; triggers fire.
• TRUNCATE (DDL): Removes all rows from a table rapidly by deallocating pages; cannot be rolled back easily; preserves table structure.
• DROP (DDL): Completely removes the entire table structure, indexes, and data permanently from the database schema.
Q40. What is the difference between COUNT(*) and COUNT(column_name) in SQL?
• COUNT(*): Counts every single row in the table, including rows where certain columns contain NULL values.
• COUNT(column): Counts only rows where the specified column contains non-NULL values (ignores NULLs).
Q41. What is the explode parameter in Matplotlib pie charts?
In plt.pie(), the explode parameter accepts a sequence of fractions (e.g., [0.1, 0, 0, 0]) indicating the fraction of the radius with which each wedge is offset outwards from the center of the pie, highlighting or emphasizing a specific sector.
Q42. What is Referential Integrity in relational databases? How is it enforced?
Referential integrity is a database constraint ensuring that relationships between tables remain consistent. It dictates that any foreign key value in a child table must match an existing primary key value in the referenced parent table, or must be NULL. It prevents orphaned records.
Q43. What is the difference between active and passive digital footprints?
• Active Digital Footprint: Data intentionally submitted online by the user (e.g., posting photos on Instagram, sending emails, writing blog comments).
• Passive Digital Footprint: Data collected unintentionally without the user's active input (e.g., browsing history, IP address tracking via web cookies, device geolocation logs).
