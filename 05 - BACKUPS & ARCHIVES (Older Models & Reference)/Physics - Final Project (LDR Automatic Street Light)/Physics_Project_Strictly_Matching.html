AIM
To design, assemble, and analyze an energy-saving Automatic Street Light Control Circuit utilizing a Light Dependent Resistor (LDR) sensor and a BC547 NPN Bipolar Junction Transistor operating in digital switching mode.
Primary Research Objectives:
  • To construct a voltage divider network coupling an LDR with a variable/fixed resistance.
  • To demonstrate the automatic illumination of a street light LED when ambient light drops below a threshold level.
  • To study the V-I characteristics, transistor cutoff and saturation states, and photo-conductive behavior of Cadmium Sulphide (CdS).
6
APPARATUS REQUIRED
  1. Light Dependent Resistor (LDR): 5 mm standard Cadmium Sulphide (CdS) photoresistor.
  2. BC547 NPN Transistor: Bipolar Junction Transistor in standard TO-92 plastic package.
  3. Light Emitting Diode (LED): High-brightness 5 mm white LED (simulating street lamp).
  4. Current-Limiting Resistor (330 Ω, 0.25W): Connected in series with LED anode.
  5. Base Bias Resistor / Potentiometer (10 kΩ Preset Trimpot): For voltage division.
  6. Power Source: 9V Alkaline Battery (PP3 heavy duty) with snap connector.
  7. Solderless Breadboard / PCB Mounting Board: For secure component wiring.
  8. Connecting Jumper Wires: Single-strand insulated copper connecting leads.

BACKGROUND

Municipal street lighting accounts for an enormous fraction of worldwide electrical energy consumption. In conventional municipal configurations, street lamps are either toggled manually by maintenance crews or governed by crude mechanical timers. Both paradigms suffer chronic operational inefficiencies: lights remain illuminated during broad daylight hours due to human neglect, wasting mega-watts of grid energy.

Why Solid-State Automated Switching is Superior:
  • Autonomous Response: Senses actual radiant daylight intensity rather than arbitrary clock times, responding dynamically to overcast monsoon skies and solar eclipses.
  • Zero Energy Wastage: Extinguishes street lamps instantly when ambient lux levels exceed daylight threshold, slashing municipal grid load.
  • Solid-State Reliability: Contains no mechanical relays, bimetallic strips, or moving gears, eliminating mechanical wear and contact arcing.
  • Low Capital Cost: Can be deployed universally across existing solar or grid-tied infrastructure with inexpensive discrete semiconductor components.

WORKING PRINCIPLE
1. Photoconductivity of Cadmium Sulphide (CdS):

An LDR operates on the internal photoelectric effect. In darkness, valence electrons are bound to lattice atoms, yielding an enormous dark resistance (500 kΩ to 2 MΩ). When incident daylight photons strike the CdS surface with energy greater than the bandgap:

hν > Eg ⟶ Electron-Hole Pairs Generated ⟶ Resistance Collapses (~1 kΩ)

2. Voltage Divider and Transistor Switching:

The LDR and 10 kΩ preset form a voltage divider across the 9V supply. The base voltage Vbe is governed by:

Vbase = Vsupply × [ Rpreset / (RLDR + Rpreset) ]

  • Daylight Condition: RLDR ≈ 1 kΩ. Vbase < 0.6V. The BC547 is in CUTOFF (open switch). Lamp is OFF.
  • Darkness Condition: RLDR > 500 kΩ. Vbase > 0.7V. The BC547 is in SATURATION (closed switch). Lamp is ON.

PROCEDURE
  1. Place the solderless breadboard on a level workbench and identify the positive (+) and negative (-) common bus strips.
  2. Insert the BC547 NPN transistor into three adjacent terminal rows with its flat face oriented towards you.
  3. Connect the Emitter pin (Pin 3) directly to the ground negative power rail using a short black jumper lead.
  4. Insert the 5 mm LDR between the +9V supply rail and the transistor Base pin (Pin 2).
  5. Insert the 10 kΩ resistor between the Base pin (Pin 2) and the ground negative rail, completing the potential divider network.
  6. Connect the 330 Ω resistor from the +9V rail to the LED anode (longer leg). Connect the LED cathode (shorter leg) to the transistor Collector (Pin 1).
  7. Connect the 9V battery snap to the rails. Test the circuit under room illumination (LED remains OFF).
  8. Cover the LDR completely with an opaque object or your finger (LED instantly illuminates brightly).

RESULT & DISCUSSION
1. Final Experimental Result:

The Automatic Street Light Control Circuit utilizing an LDR and BC547 NPN transistor switch was successfully designed, constructed, and experimentally validated. The circuit demonstrates flawless automatic illumination under darkness and automatic extinction under daylight, eliminating the necessity for human intervention.

2. Technical Discussion:

The experimental data confirms that Cadmium Sulphide exhibits excellent spectral responsiveness within visible light wavelengths (500 nm to 600 nm). The sensitivity threshold is easily adjustable via the 10 kΩ trimmer, enabling custom calibration for residential lanes, highways, or security courtyards.

3. Industrial Scalability:

To control high-voltage 230V AC municipal lamps (sodium vapor or halogen luminaires), the low-voltage transistor collector output can seamlessly drive an electromagnetic relay or a solid-state TRIAC interfaced with an optocoupler.

SOURCES OF ERROR
  • Battery Internal Resistance: Gradual electrochemical discharge of the 9V alkaline battery during extended testing causes terminal voltage to drop below 8.0V, diminishing LED lumen output.
  • False Optical Triggering: Passing vehicular headlights or reflective surfaces striking the LDR during night hours may cause momentary flickering without adequate optical shielding.
  • Breadboard Contact Resistance: Loose spring clips inside economy breadboards can introduce parasitic resistance, shifting the calibrated voltage divider threshold.
  • Thermal Drift in Silicon BJT: Ambient room temperature elevation increases reverse saturation leakage current (Icbo), marginally altering the base threshold voltage.
14
PRECAUTIONS
  1. Current-Limiting Protection: Never connect the LED directly across the 9V battery without the 330 Ω series resistor; excessive forward current will destroy the semiconductor junction.
  2. Correct Transistor Pinout: Double-check Collector, Base, and Emitter pin orientation against the BC547 datasheet before applying power.
  3. Power Conservation: Disconnect the 9V battery clip immediately after taking readings to avoid unnecessary drain before viva examination.
  4. Optical Isolation: Shield the LDR physically from the street light LED itself to prevent optical feedback oscillations (where the lamp turns on, blinds the sensor, and turns itself off in an endless loop).
  5. Terminal Security: Ensure all jumper wires are firmly seated inside breadboard tie points to prevent intermittent connections.
