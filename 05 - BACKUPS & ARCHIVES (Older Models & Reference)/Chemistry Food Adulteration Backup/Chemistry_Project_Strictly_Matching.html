AIM

To study and detect the presence of common adulterants in daily food items including starch in milk, chalk powder and washing soda in cane sugar, metanil yellow in turmeric powder, and lead chromate in chilli powder.
Food Items Tested in this Project:
  • Dairy Products: Packaged milk, loose local dairy milk, and butter.
  • Sweeteners: Commercial refined white sugar and unbranded sugar.
  • Spices: Branded packaged turmeric powder, loose turmeric powder, and loose red chilli powder.

  • Test Tubes: Clean Borosil glass test tubes (15 mL) and boiling tubes.
  • Test Tube Stand & Holder: Wooden stand for holding tubes and metallic holder for gentle heating.
  • Glass Beakers: 100 mL and 250 mL beakers for preparing sample solutions.
  • Glass Funnel & Filter Paper: Standard glass funnel with Whatman filter paper for filtration.
  • Glass Stirring Rod: Clean glass rod for dissolving solid samples and stirring test mixtures.

  • Bunsen Burner: Gas burner with tripod stand and wire gauze for boiling milk and heating solutions.
  • Measuring Cylinder & Droppers: 25 mL measuring cylinder and glass droppers for adding exact acid drops.

CHEMICALS REQUIRED

  • Dilute Hydrochloric Acid (Dil. HCl): Used for testing effervescence of carbonates from chalk and washing soda in sugar.
  • Concentrated Hydrochloric Acid (Conc. HCl): Used for testing the non-permitted yellow coal-tar dye (Metanil Yellow) in turmeric powder.
  • Iodine Solution (0.05M I₂ in KI): Used for detecting starch in milk and butter by formation of an intense blue-black colour.
  • Concentrated Nitric Acid (Conc. HNO₃): Used for extracting lead and chromate adulterants from chilli powder.
  • Potassium Iodide Solution (10% KI): Used to confirm lead ions by precipitating bright yellow lead iodide (PbI₂).
  • Fresh Lime Water (Ca(OH)₂ solution): Used to confirm carbon dioxide gas evolved from chalk powder.
  • Distilled Water: Used for preparing sample suspensions and washing glassware.
  • Daily Food Samples: Milk, butter, sugar, turmeric powder, and red chilli powder.

THEORY / BACKGROUND

Food is essential for our survival, growth, and health. However, some dishonest traders and food manufacturers add cheaper, substandard, or toxic substances to food items to increase weight, volume, or visual appeal. This practice is known as Food Adulteration.

Adulteration lowers the nutritional quality of food and can cause severe health hazards. For instance, consuming toxic dyes like metanil yellow can damage the liver, while lead chromate in spices can lead to chronic lead poisoning, anaemia, and kidney failure.

Scientific Basis of Chemical Detection Tests:
  • Starch in Milk: Iodine molecules fit into the coil structure of starch polymers (amylose), producing an intense deep blue-black complex. Pure milk contains only lactose and fats, so it does not give a blue colour.
  • Chalk Powder in Sugar: Chalk contains insoluble calcium carbonate (CaCO₃). Adding dilute HCl produces brisk effervescence of CO₂ gas:
    CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂↑
  • Metanil Yellow in Turmeric: Pure natural turmeric (curcumin) turns bright red with concentrated HCl, but this colour disappears immediately upon adding water. Metanil yellow produces a persistent magenta-pink colour that does not fade when diluted.
  • Lead Chromate in Chilli: Lead ions react with potassium iodide to precipitate bright yellow lead iodide:
    Pb²⁺ + 2I⁻ → PbI₂ ↓ (Yellow Precipitate)

EXPERIMENT - 1

Aim: To detect the presence of starch in milk and butter.

Why Starch is Added: Starch (such as corn flour, arrowroot, or potato powder) is often added to diluted milk and butter by local sellers to increase thickness and make it look rich in solids.

Procedure:
  1. Take approximately 5 mL of the milk sample in a clean boiling tube.
  2. Heat the milk gently over a Bunsen burner flame until it comes to a boil, then allow it to cool down to room temperature.
  3. Add 2 to 3 drops of iodine reagent using a glass dropper.
  4. Mix the contents gently and observe any colour change.
  5. Repeat the same test with a small piece of melted butter dissolved in warm water.
Observations:
  • Sample A (Branded Packaged Milk): The mixture retained the light yellowish-brown tint of iodine. No blue colour appeared. → Starch is absent.
  • Sample B (Loose Local Dairy Milk): The mixture turned an immediate, intense dark blue-black colour. → Adulterated with starch.
  • Sample C (Butter): Did not show any blue-black coloration. → Pure sample.

EXPERIMENT - 2

Aim: To detect chalk powder and washing soda in cane sugar.

Why Chalk & Soda are Added: Fine white chalk powder and washing soda are mixed into cane sugar to increase bulk weight and mimic the appearance of white sugar crystals.

Procedure:
  1. Take approximately 10 grams of the sugar sample and dissolve it in 50 mL of clean distilled water in a beaker.
  2. Stir thoroughly with a glass rod. Pure cane sugar dissolves completely into a clear, transparent solution.
  3. Observe whether any white insoluble residue settles at the bottom of the beaker.
  4. Filter the solution using a funnel and Whatman filter paper. Collect the residue.
  5. Add a few drops of dilute Hydrochloric Acid (Dil. HCl) directly onto the residue on the filter paper.
  6. Test the escaping gas by passing it through freshly prepared lime water.
Observations:
  • Sample A (Branded Sugar): Dissolved completely into a clear liquid. No residue remained on the filter paper. → Free from insoluble adulterants.
  • Sample B (Loose Market Sugar): A fine white powder remained insoluble at the bottom. Adding dilute HCl caused brisk effervescence of carbon dioxide gas, which turned lime water milky. → Adulterated with chalk powder (CaCO₃).
11

EXPERIMENT - 3

Aim: To detect metanil yellow dye in turmeric powder.

Why Metanil Yellow is Added: Metanil yellow is an artificial, industrial azo dye used in textiles. Unscrupulous spice vendors add it to low-quality, dull turmeric powder to impart a bright golden-yellow colour.

Procedure:
  1. Take approximately 1 gram of turmeric powder in a clean, dry test tube.
  2. Add 5 mL of pure distilled water and shake the test tube vigorously to create a uniform yellow suspension.
  3. Add 3 to 4 drops of Concentrated Hydrochloric Acid (Conc. HCl) along the inner side of the test tube.
  4. Observe the immediate colour change.
  5. Now add approximately 5 mL of distilled water to dilute the mixture and note whether the pink/magenta colour remains or fades away.
Observations:
  • Sample A (Branded Packaged Turmeric): Developed a temporary red tinge with conc. HCl, which disappeared completely when water was added, returning to the normal yellow colour. → Pure natural turmeric.
  • Sample B (Loose Local Market Turmeric): An intense, vivid magenta-pink colour formed instantly with conc. HCl. When diluted with water, the bright pink colour persisted without fading. → Adulterated with Metanil Yellow dye.

EXPERIMENT - 4

Aim: To detect lead chromate and red lead in chilli powder.

Why Lead Chromate is Added: Lead chromate (PbCrO₄) and brick powder are added to low-grade chilli powder to give it a vibrant red colour and add heavy weight.

Procedure:
  1. Take about 2 grams of the red chilli powder sample in a 100 mL beaker.
  2. Add 10 mL of pure distilled water. Pure chilli powder floats on the surface, while heavy mineral adulterants like brick dust settle quickly to the bottom.
  3. In a second test tube, take 1 gram of chilli powder and add 5 mL of Concentrated Nitric Acid (Conc. HNO₃).
  4. Warm the test tube gently over a low flame for 2 minutes and filter the solution into another clean test tube.
  5. Add a few drops of 10% Potassium Iodide (KI) solution to the clear filtrate.
  6. Observe whether a bright yellow precipitate forms.
Observations:
  • Sample A (Packaged Branded Chilli): Powder floated on water with minimal sediment. Adding KI produced no yellow precipitate. → Pure sample.
  • Sample B (Loose Market Chilli Powder): Gritty red particles settled at the bottom. Adding KI solution produced a distinct, bright yellow precipitate of Lead Iodide (PbI₂). → Adulterated with toxic Lead Chromate.

PRECAUTIONS

Laboratory Safety Precautions:
  • Always handle Concentrated Hydrochloric Acid (Conc. HCl) and Concentrated Nitric Acid (Conc. HNO₃) carefully using clean droppers. Do not inhale their fumes.
  • Wash all test tubes and beakers thoroughly with pure distilled water before each test so previous residues do not give false positives.
  • When heating milk or chemical mixtures in a test tube, hold the tube at an angle pointing away from yourself and other students.
  • Ensure lime water is freshly prepared and clear; otherwise, it will not give a sharp test with carbon dioxide gas.
Simple Tips for Everyday Consumers:
  • Prefer buying spices, pulses, and milk in sealed packets bearing AGMARK, FSSAI, or ISI certification marks.
  • Avoid buying unbranded loose spices and oils from open street markets with unusually low prices.
  • A simple home test for chilli powder: drop a teaspoon into a glass of water. Pure chilli stays on top, while brick powder or artificial colours quickly sink, leaving red streaks.

CONCLUSION

1. Results & Findings:

  • Branded packaged samples of milk, sugar, and turmeric were found to be completely genuine and free from foreign adulterants.
