AIM & INTRODUCTION

1. AIM OF THE INVESTIGATION

To assemble and study the working of a Step-Down Electrical Transformer, verify the relationship between coil turns and voltages (Vs / Vp = Ns / Np), and calculate its efficiency under resistive load.

2. PROJECT OBJECTIVES

  • To study the principle of Mutual Induction between two inductively coupled coils.
  • To verify the turns-to-voltage relationship for primary and secondary windings.
  • To measure input power and output power, and calculate the operating efficiency.
  • To examine the four major power losses in transformers and how they are reduced in practice.

3. INTRODUCTION

Almost all electricity in our homes and industries is supplied as alternating current (AC). One major advantage of AC over DC is that AC voltage can be stepped up or stepped down easily using a transformer.
Power stations generate electricity and step it up to very high voltages (like 132 kV or 400 kV) so that the current is reduced, which minimizes I²R heat losses during long-distance transmission. When the electricity reaches cities and houses, substations step it down to 220 V. Devices like mobile chargers, computer adapters, and laboratory battery eliminators use step-down transformers to bring 220 V down to safe working levels such as 12 V or 5 V.

PRINCIPLE & THEORY

WORKING PRINCIPLE: MUTUAL INDUCTION

A transformer is a static electrical device with no moving parts. It transfers electrical power from one circuit to another at the same frequency, while changing the voltage and current.
It works on the principle of Mutual Induction. When alternating current flows through the primary winding, it produces a magnetic field that changes direction and strength 50 times every second (for 50 Hz AC).
This changing magnetic flux passes through the soft-iron core and links with the secondary coil. According to Faraday’s Law of Electromagnetic Induction, a changing magnetic flux induces an alternating voltage (EMF) across the secondary coil terminals.
e = - M • (dI / dt)
Here, M is the coefficient of mutual inductance between the two coils, and (dI / dt) is the rate of change of primary current.
7

MATHEMATICAL THEORY

MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION & EQUATIONS

Let Np = Number of turns in the primary coil, and Ns = Number of turns in the secondary coil. Let Φ be the magnetic flux linked with each turn of the core at any instant.
By Faraday’s law of induction, the induced EMF in the primary coil is:
Ep = - Np • (dΦ / dt)
Similarly, the induced EMF in the secondary coil is:
Es = - Ns • (dΦ / dt)
Assuming zero flux leakage and negligible resistance in ideal coils, Ep ≈ Vp and Es ≈ Vs. Dividing the two equations gives:
Vs / Vp = Ns / Np = K
Where K is called the Transformation Ratio.
• For a Step-Down Transformer: Ns < Np ⇒ K < 1 and Vs < Vp.
• By the Law of Conservation of Energy, for an ideal transformer (100% efficient):
Input Power = Output Power ⇒ Vp • Ip = Vs • Is
This gives: Is / Ip = Vp / Vs = 1 / K.
Therefore, as voltage is stepped down, current is stepped up proportionately (Is > Ip).

PROCEDURE

STEP-BY-STEP ASSEMBLY AND TESTING

Step 1 (Core Preparation): We inspected the silicon steel E and I stampings. Each lamination has a thin coating of varnish to prevent electrical contact between adjacent plates.
Step 2 (Primary Winding): We wound approximately 1000 turns of thin 28 SWG enamelled copper wire around the central limb of the plastic bobbin. The wire was wound tightly in neat, even layers. After completing 1000 turns, we wrapped two layers of insulating tape over it.
Step 3 (Secondary Winding): Over the insulated primary layer, we wound 100 turns of thicker 20 SWG copper wire. Thicker wire was chosen because the secondary current is higher, which keeps heating low.
Step 4 (Core Assembly): We inserted the E and I stampings alternately into the bobbin from opposite sides to create a closed magnetic loop. We clamped the frame tightly using screws to prevent buzzing noise.
Step 5 (Testing & Readings): We connected the primary to an AC supply and connected a 12 V bulb across the secondary. Using digital multimeters, we recorded Vp, Ip, Vs, and Is at five different input voltage levels.

CALCULATIONS

POWER & EFFICIENCY CALCULATIONS

Taking the values from Observation Reading 1:
Input Voltage (Vp) = 220.0 V, Input Current (Ip) = 0.120 A
Output Voltage (Vs) = 21.2 V, Output Current (Is) = 1.10 A
1. Input Electrical Power (Pin):
Pin = Vp • Ip = 220.0 V × 0.120 A = 26.40 Watts
2. Output Electrical Power (Pout):
Pout = Vs • Is = 21.2 V × 1.10 A = 23.32 Watts
3. Power Loss in Transformer (Ploss):
Ploss = Pin - Pout = 26.40 W - 23.32 W = 3.08 Watts
4. Transformation Efficiency (η):
η = (Output Power / Input Power) × 100%
η = (23.32 / 26.40) × 100% = 88.33%
Average Practical Efficiency (η_avg) ≈ 88.1%
The efficiency of around 88% is typical for small laboratory transformers of this size, where small coil heating and core losses are present.

ENERGY LOSSES

MAJOR LOSSES AND HOW TO MINIMIZE THEM

In a real transformer, output power is always less than input power due to four main losses:
1. Copper Loss (I²R Loss):
The copper wire has some electrical resistance. Current flowing through the coils produces heat energy (I²R).
How to minimize: Use thicker copper wire on the secondary winding where current is higher, which keeps resistance low.
2. Eddy Current Loss:
The alternating magnetic flux in the iron core induces circulating currents (eddy currents), which produce heat in the iron.
How to minimize: Build the core from thin, varnished sheets (laminations) instead of a solid iron block. The varnish breaks the path of the circulating currents.
3. Hysteresis Loss:
The iron core is magnetized and demagnetized 50 times every second. Repeated reversal of magnetic domains takes energy, which is released as heat.
How to minimize: Use high-permeability soft iron or silicon steel, which has a very narrow hysteresis loop.
4. Flux Leakage:
Not all magnetic lines produced by the primary pass through the secondary coil; some leak through the air.
How to minimize: Wind the secondary coil directly on top of the primary coil on the central limb of the core.

PRECAUTIONS

SAFETY MEASURES & SOURCES OF ERROR

A. SAFETY PRECAUTIONS:
  • All 220 V primary connections must be properly insulated using heat-shrink sleeves or tape to avoid electric shock.
  • Never connect Direct Current (DC) to a transformer. Since DC does not change, there is zero inductive reactance, leading to very large current that will burn the primary coil.
  • Always clamp the E-I core laminations firmly with bolts to prevent humming noise and magnetic flux loss.
  • Do not touch the coils during continuous operation as the windings can become warm.
B. SOURCES OF EXPERIMENTAL ERROR:
  • Mains AC supply voltage fluctuates slightly (±5 V) during the test.
  • Small air gaps between the E and I laminations reduce ideal magnetic flux linkage.
  • Resistance in connecting wires and clip terminals causes a small voltage drop.
  • Meters have internal resistance and a small calibration tolerance.
